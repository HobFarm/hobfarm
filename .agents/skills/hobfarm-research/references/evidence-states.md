# HobFarm Evidence States

Assign each material claim the state that best describes how it is known.

| State | Meaning | Public treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Documented fact | A reliable source directly establishes the claim | State plainly and cite where the form benefits from a receipt |
| Author-confirmed identification | The author identifies a person, place, object, work, or event from direct knowledge | State naturally; keep author provenance in source notes when useful |
| Firsthand observation | The author directly saw, heard, tried, measured, photographed, or experienced it | Use first person or an attributed observation |
| Recollection | The author remembers it with the supplied confidence | Preserve markers such as “I remember,” “I think,” or an approximate date |
| Visual comparison | Identification or change follows from comparing images, maps, frames, or physical features | State what the visual supports and keep the comparison record |
| Inference | Several facts or observations support a conclusion | Show the bridge that produces the conclusion |
| Interpretation | The article offers a reading of the evidence | Make the evidentiary basis visible |
| Opinion | The author's judgment carries the sentence | Supply enough context for the judgment to land |
| Speculation or proposal | The piece explores a possibility, design, future, or thought experiment | Keep the frame clear and develop it imaginatively |
| Open question | Available evidence has not settled the point | Preserve the question when it remains editorially useful |

## Claim preservation

Carry every material author claim into the finished copy, source ledger, or explicit cut decision. A missing search result leaves the claim in its current evidence state. Contradictory evidence changes the state or wording and remains visible in the handoff.

Public prose should read naturally. The source ledger can carry the full provenance label when repeating it in the caption or paragraph would make the writing clumsy.
