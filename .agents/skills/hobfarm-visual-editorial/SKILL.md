# HobFarm Visual Editorial

Make every visual perform a clear editorial job while preserving the character and purpose of the author's selected material.

Read [`../../../docs/editorial/EDITORIAL_CHARTER.md`](../../../docs/editorial/EDITORIAL_CHARTER.md).

## Selected source media

Use user-supplied and user-selected media as approved working material for the requested article or page. Base descriptions, classifications, captions, and production decisions on visibly present content, the author's context, the image's editorial purpose, and actual site requirements.

Preserve the subject, composition, documentary value, humor, glamour, sexuality, grotesquerie, satire, character, and atmosphere that make the image useful. Optional editorial treatment, including badges, labels, warnings, frames, or comparable presentation, requires the user's explicit request or approval for that asset. The visible subject, external conventions, service behavior, historical practice, and tool capabilities may inform options, but they do not authorize unsolicited treatment.

Apply requirements that are technically necessary for rendering, accessibility, safety, schema compliance, or valid output. Keep those requirements distinct from optional editorial treatment.

An existing photograph is source material. A generation or editing service may impose a genuine limitation on an operation it performs. When that happens, identify the exact operation and limitation, keep the original available, and choose the closest supported route with the smallest useful adjustment.

## Identification and captions

Treat author identifications as evidence with author provenance. External corroboration can strengthen or refine them. A missing external match leaves the identification available as author-confirmed, recollected, or visually compared according to the supplied confidence.

Write captions that add information: identification, date, place, source, what changed, what the reader should notice, or a concise editorial observation. Keep full provenance in source notes when the public caption benefits from natural phrasing.

Write alt text for the visual information needed to understand the article. For charts and diagrams, summarize the relationship or trend. For documentary photographs, identify important objects, setting, and action.

## Choose the visual job

Useful jobs include establishing place, showing the object, proving change over time, explaining a mechanism, showing scale, comparing states, documenting firsthand experience, preserving an interface or artifact, carrying a joke, and establishing period atmosphere.

Use photography and archival material for direct evidence. Use diagrams, maps, charts, composites, animation, and interactive elements for relationships the reader benefits from seeing. Use generated editorial illustration for scenes, comparisons, or concepts that source media cannot show cleanly.

Track source, rights or provenance, crop, aspect ratio, caption, alt text, generation prompt when applicable, article placement, and status. Read the article once through its images and captions; the sequence should still communicate a recognizable version of the story.
