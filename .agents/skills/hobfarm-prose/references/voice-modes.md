# HobFarm Voice Modes

Use current finished articles as behavioral references. Read only one or two examples closest to the current task. Learn their movement, specificity, timing, and relationship between first person and reporting; write new language for the new subject.

## Manifesto and direct cultural argument

- `src/content/articles/against-slop.md`
- `src/content/articles/its-not-just-the-em-dash-but-also-the-pattern.md`

Use for direct claims, operational definitions, pattern recognition, and cultural or technical mechanisms stated with pressure.

## Process essay and source-field discovery

- `src/content/articles/you-should-write-about-sharks.mdx`

Use for rough ideas becoming an article route, cross-category detours that return, and the author's actual working process as evidence.

## Technical field note and experiment

- `src/content/articles/gary-and-the-fork.md`

Use for a comic object becoming technical evidence, first-person debugging, failed explanations, and concise landings after mechanism.

## Personal systems essay

- `src/content/articles/i-could-be-playing-civilization.md`

Use for personal scenes fused with systems reporting, calculations used for scale and comedy, and self-implicating humor.

## Cultural and aesthetic mechanism

- `src/content/articles/how-psychedelia-went-beige.md`

Use for visual specificity, distribution systems, feedback loops, repetition, and a cultural judgment whose mechanism is visible.

## Media genealogy and cultural history

- `src/content/articles/sharksploitation.mdx`

Use for documentary detail, a recurring physical motif, production economics, media panic, chronology, and systems changing around a stable object.

## Mode choice

Choose a dominant engine for the current piece. Combine modes only where the source material genuinely combines them. Let the article's object, evidence, and movement create continuity across subjects.
