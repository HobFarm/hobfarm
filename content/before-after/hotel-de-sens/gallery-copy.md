# Hôtel de Sens gallery copy

## Gallery introduction

Ten views follow the Hôtel de Sens from a picturesque nineteenth-century survivor through commercial use, municipal acquisition, clearance around the block, restoration, and its present life as the Bibliothèque Forney. Prints, paintings, and drawings are identified by medium. The photographs remain tied to dated institutional records.

## Before-and-after introduction

The 1920 and 2016 photographs show the same street façade from near-corresponding positions. The crops bring the turrets and pointed entrance to a similar scale. They do not create an exact lens or camera match, and no perspective correction was applied.

## Comparison captions

**Before — May 26, 1920.** The Hôtel de Sens stands inside an active commercial street. A market wall, advertisements, pedestrians, street furniture, and a car surround the dark façade. Agence Meurisse / Bibliothèque nationale de France, public domain.

**After — August 28, 2016.** The restored street front serves as the entrance to the Bibliothèque Forney. Pale masonry, repaired roofs and windows, and a clearer street edge replace the crowded 1920 setting. Guilhem Vellut, CC BY 2.0.

## One-sentence sequence captions

1. **1839:** Thomas Shotter Boys treats a corner turret as a picturesque Paris survivor; this is a lithograph, not a photograph.
2. **1869:** The Confiturerie de Saint James sign makes commercial occupation visible above the main entrance.
3. **c. 1880:** An anonymous winter painting records the broad façade and street setting without claiming photographic precision.
4. **1898–1900:** Eugène Atget turns along the side street, showing how closely neighboring buildings met the old house.
5. **1911:** An Agence Rol photograph records the hemmed-in façade in the year Paris bought the building.
6. **1920:** Agence Meurisse captures the supplied before scene: market wall, shopfront, signs, car, and pedestrians around the unrestored hotel.
7. **1926:** Gustave Ferdinand Boberg draws demolished houses opposite the monument; the image concerns the surrounding block, not repairs to the hotel.
8. **1928–29:** A stereoscopic view records the façade at the threshold of restoration but does not prove a particular construction operation.
9. **2016:** Guilhem Vellut's frontal photograph provides the modern after frame and the clearest comparison of the paired turrets and entrance.
10. **2017:** Bobo Boom's oblique view adds modern street context and makes the effect of camera position easy to see.

## Social caption

The Hôtel de Sens survived as Paris changed its owners, uses, walls, streets, and idea of what an old building was for. Ten documented views, from 1839 to 2017, show the difference between a building that persisted and a scene that did not.
