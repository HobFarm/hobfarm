- JamBase lists Toots and the Maytals at Monument Square in Portland, Maine, on July 29, 2004: https://www.jambase.com/band/toots-and-the-maytals/setlists/2004

## Portland graffiti and the three branches

- The Bollard's October 2006 Tim Clorius interview calls him a 30-year-old German-American from Heidelberg, documents youth mural workshops and his transition from spray paint to oil, and says the anti-war Asylum mural had been largely intact for more than three years. Do not date the mural's first version to July 29, 2004: https://thebollard.com/2006/10/01/subone-speaks-out/
- The Portland Public Library's index of a September 2003 Portland Monthly feature identifies Faze as Eli Cayer, Bob Smith, and Tim Clorius, all with graffiti roots: https://digitalcommons.portlandlibrary.com/news_portmonthly/549/
- A 2011 Press Herald history says Mike Rich proposed the Asylum wall in 1997, became its de facto curator, and notes the earlier George W. Bush/Armageddon mural: https://www.pressherald.com/2011/10/14/portland-mural-its-a-wall-marked-card/
- Mike Rich's current site describes him as a Portland multidisciplinary artist specializing in graffiti and large-scale murals with more than thirty years in the form: https://mikerichart.com/
- A September 2021 Press Herald report and photo show Mike Rich and Tim Clorius painting over anti-Black and anti-gay slurs behind the Lancaster Building/Aura: https://www.pressherald.com/2021/09/03/portland-police-investigating-vandalism-of-downtown-murals/
- The Maine Mural Festival's 2026 page lists Tim Clorius among its artists. It does not supply a birth date: https://www.thecolorwizard.com/mainemuralfestival
- The 2006 Bollard age is enough to support that Tim was born in 1976 and turns 50 in 2026, without inventing an exact birthday.

## Eli Cayer and MENSK

- The Portland Public Library index of an August 2001 Casco Bay Weekly item says Eli worked for more than a year to gain approval for a free graffiti wall at the East End Treatment Facility: https://digitalcommons.portlandlibrary.com/news_cbw/4251/
- MENSK describes itself as a fiscal sponsor for small or time-bound local projects, with creative/sustainable communities and event logistics at the center. Its archive lists Portland Rooftop Films: https://www.menskmaine.org/
- The Press Herald's 2011 rooftop-film account documents free screenings on the Spring Street garage and MENSK's sponsorship: https://www.pressherald.com/2011/06/30/rooftop-film-series-pee-wee-and-oh-so-easy-parking_2011-06-30/
- A Terra Firma profile says Eli began keeping bees and making mead in 2002, opened Maine Mead Works in 2007, stepped away in 2010, and founded Urban Farm Fermentory in early 2010: https://www.terrafirmamagazine.com/case-studies/eli-cayer-urban-farm-fermentory/
- Urban Farm Fermentory ended its Portland run in 2024. A contemporary closure account preserves Eli's description of the place as a community, education, and gathering experiment: https://www.boochnews.com/2024/04/12/rip-urban-farm-fermentory-portland-maine/
- Bangor Daily News/The County reported in June 2026 that Eli Cayer and Jason Dionne were building the community-funded NorthStar Brewpub in Madawaska's former border station: https://www.bangordailynews.com/2026/06/23/aroostook/madawaska-brewpub-crowdfunding-joam40zk0w/

## Broadcast and YouTube transition

- The 2008 Television & Cable Factbook lists WCSH-DT beginning operation April 29, 2002. It is a trade directory, not a station press release: https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-BC-YB/2008-TV-Factbook/TV-Factbook-2008-IOA-OHI.pdf
- FCC records document the June 12, 2009 full-power digital transition. This supports the wider analog/digital overlap, not a claim about a specific WCSH production workflow: https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-09-47A1.pdf
- Bangor Daily News reported in September 2009 that Scott Sassone had worked for WCSH/WLBZ as a "backpack journalist" for three years and handled photography, reporting, editing, and web reporting. The author's earlier WCSH opportunity remains first-person memory: https://www.bangordailynews.com/2009/09/30/sports/versatility-leads-sassone-to-comcast-sports-post/
- YouTube's official history dates its first upload to April 23, 2005: https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-to-z-happybirthdayyoutube/
- Comscore measured an average 100 million YouTube streams per day in July 2006: https://ir.comscore.com/news-releases/news-release-details/comscore-data-confirms-reports-100-million-worldwide-daily-video
- Google's October 9, 2006 announcement says it agreed to acquire YouTube for $1.65 billion in stock: https://googlepress.blogspot.com/2006/10/google-to-acquire-youtube-for-165_09.html

## KPVM epilogue
