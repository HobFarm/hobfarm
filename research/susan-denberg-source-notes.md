# Susan Denberg source notes

## Editorial finding

The August 1966 *Playboy* artifact catches Susan Denberg at the wrong end of its own prediction. It sells a beginning while the surviving screen chronology is already collapsing toward an ending. That is the article’s strongest documented argument.

The supplied page uses the section label *Picture Playmate*. The “dream” language appears in the body copy, not the display heading. The working title in the task packet probably inherited the phrase from the profile or from Alan Barnes’s later Hammer article. The final title should not represent it as the Playboy feature’s verified headline.

## Supplied Playboy profile: working summary

The profile presents Denberg as the latest Playmate moving into film. It announces her role as Ruta in Warner Bros.’ *An American Dream*, calls her 22, identifies Klagenfurt as her home, and describes her route through London’s Bluebells and the Stardust. The feature does not mention Bad Polzin.

It attributes her move into the Bluebells to childhood ballet training, says she stayed in the United States after the company’s Stardust engagement, and reduces the ambition to “every young girl’s dream: a movie career.” That short phrase is the only profile quotation retained for the public article.

The Warner section turns her accent and nationality into publicity shorthand for Ruta. It reports a $500 studio naming contest, more than 5,000 entries, and the decision to retain Susan Denberg. Denberg identifies *Norma Mailer* as the funniest proposal.

The closing section says she studied at the Desilu Studio Workshop and took voice and diction lessons from Gertrude Fogler. It rounds out the launch portrait with beach trips, discothèques, skiing, auto competition, and a hoped-for Corvette after more films arrived.

The scan captions place Denberg with director Robert Gist and Stuart Whitman on the Warner Bros. lot, with diction teacher Gertrude Fogler, shopping in Beverly Hills, reading a script in bed, and at a pool. Peter Gowland receives the visible photography credit.

## Image decision

The Playboy photographs are copyrighted glamour/editorial work credited to Peter Gowland. Owning or supplying scans does not itself transfer the photographic copyright. The U.S. Copyright Office identifies criticism and commentary as possible fair-use purposes but requires a case-specific four-factor analysis. A full pictorial or centerfold would reproduce the marketable core of the feature and is unnecessary for this article.

The public article therefore:

- describes the profile’s page design and uses short quotations;
- does not reproduce the nude centerfold or full page scans;
- uses a U.S.-public-domain frame from Hammer’s 1967 double-bill trailer as the hero;
- uses a Library of Congress Stardust photograph with “no known restrictions” for historical context;
- links Esquire’s official archive rather than copying installment pages;
- reserves one small Playboy page excerpt for a later editorial decision if the author supplies a local original and accepts a documented fair-use rationale.

## Publication wording

Use:

> Susan Denberg was born on August 2, 1944. No credible recent obituary or death record surfaced in this research. On the available public record, August 2, 2026 appears to be her 82nd birthday.

Do not convert absence of a death record into a claim that she is currently alive, well, or living at a particular address.
