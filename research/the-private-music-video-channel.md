# Next article seed: the private music-video channel

The recommendation trail in “The Number One Song I’ve Never Heard” stops being only a music-discovery system. It becomes a private film program built from narrative shorts, collage animation, choreography, production design, performance, visual effects, and surreal comedy.

## Working thesis

Music video did not disappear when MTV and chart television stopped functioning as shared music-video channels. It escaped the schedule and became an enormous searchable and recommendable film archive.

## Strong openings

- Salvatore Ganacci’s “Horse,” discovered through a random Spotify video playlist and now one of the author’s favorite videos.
- LCD Soundsystem’s “oh baby,” a Rian Johnson science-fiction tragedy with Sissy Spacek and David Strathairn.
- Jamie xx’s “Gosh,” using Tianducheng, an Eiffel Tower replica, mass choreography, and architectural scale.
- Bonobo’s “Cirrus,” where Cyriak turns consumer imagery into an infinitely mutating machine.

## Initial trail

Start with Sonic Youth and the author’s memory of watching these videos when they were new. Use “Cannonball” by the Breeders as a 1993 bridge. Follow real recommendation history through the Pixies, LCD Soundsystem, the Stone Roses, Dinosaur Jr., Violent Femmes, King Krule, Blur, My Bloody Valentine, the Flaming Lips, Wet Leg, Water From Your Eyes, Jamie xx, Bonobo, and Salvatore Ganacci.

Additional cases must come from the author’s actual watch history, not a generic best-of list.

## Article question

What can a music video do that a feature film, social clip, concert recording, or conventional promotional video cannot?

Research director and production credits, official video sources, production accounts, awards through primary sources, rights and embed status, and the historical bridge from *Top of the Pops* and MTV to personalized video discovery. Do not draft the article until that research and another real watch-history sample exist.
