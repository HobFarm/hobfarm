# Keep the Character: course draft

This is the complete editorial draft for operator testing. It is not a public paid body and is not approved for sale. Exact provider interface steps remain `OPERATOR_INPUT_REQUIRED` where the source record does not prove them.

## Free preview 1: What Belongs to the Character?

Solve: the learner treats the entire source image as identity.  
Build: an identity/wardrobe boundary list.

Use the blonde and green Workshop examples. Divide observed traits into identity, wardrobe, style, scene, and motion. Mark every trait that appears only in a later generated sheet as an inference. The independent variation repeats the classification with a learner-owned source.

Checkpoint: another reader can replace the wardrobe description without rewriting the identity record.

## Free preview 2: Read the Failure Before Fixing It

Solve: a generated sheet guesses unseen clothing and the guess is later treated as canon.  
Build: a failure annotation.

Place the source and generated sheet together. Mark what is directly visible, what remains consistent, what was inferred, and what drifted. Do not repair yet. The decision is whether the output provides evidence or merely a plausible invention.

Checkpoint: the annotation names the unsupported clothing guess and keeps it outside the identity lock.

## Paid 1: Define the Source and the Job

Solve: the production begins without a bounded source or output.  
Build: a character source record.

Record the source owner, rights/consent state, visible views, target output, required continuity, allowed invention, prohibited invention, tool route, and stop condition. Mark absent views as absent. The guided exercise uses one HobFarm source; the independent exercise uses the learner's source.

Failure check: a goal such as “make a consistent character” is too broad. Replace it with the exact comparison or production board needed now.

Checkpoint: the record lets another person tell what may change and when the test should stop.

## Paid 2: Write the Identity Lock

Solve: clothing, rendering style, and scene details are being recorded as character identity.  
Build: an identity-lock file.

Record supported face geometry, hair, proportions, palette, marks, makeup, seams, stitches, drip placement, and other stable traits. Keep garments, props, lighting, camera, environment, and rendering treatment in separate fields. Add an uncertainty field for each trait not confirmed by the source.

Failure check: if the identity record cannot survive a wardrobe swap, it contains wardrobe.

`OPERATOR_INPUT_REQUIRED`: approve the exact HobFarm identity-lock example after the clean walkthrough.

## Paid 3: Remove Wardrobe and Scene Noise

Solve: the base carries outfit and environment cues into every variation.  
Build: a neutral clean base or mannequin.

Begin with the identity lock, not the styled output. Direct the minimum neutral view that removes clothing and environmental inference while retaining supported traits. Compare against the source at each accepted step. Stop when further simplification begins to erase identity evidence.

Failure check: a blank mannequin that no longer carries the supported face, hair, proportions, marks, or material behavior is not a successful neutral base.

`OPERATOR_INPUT_REQUIRED`: record the actual provider/date, failed neutralization, and correction. Do not invent a prompt.

## Paid 4: Build Usable Views

Solve: a multi-view sheet invents unsupported angles and treats them as facts.  
Build: the smallest production sheet the source can support.

Choose front, profile, rear, or detail views only when the source and job require them. Label generated transitions and uncertain regions. Do not let a complete-looking sheet hide missing evidence. Save the source, accepted views, rejected views, and uncertainty notes together.

Checkpoint: every view is marked source-supported, inferred, or rejected.

## Paid 5: Same Character, Two Looks

Solve: wardrobe and scene changes are altering the base identity.  
Build: two same-base styling tests.

Define outfit A and outfit B as separate assets. Record garment shape, material, accessories, palette, and scene direction without copying those traits into the identity lock. Apply each to the same accepted base. Compare identity traits first, then styling traits.

Failure check: a prettier image that changes the face, hair structure, proportions, marks, or other locked evidence fails the continuity test.

Checkpoint: the comparison says what changed, what stayed, and what must be repaired.

## Paid 6: Same Look, Different Character

Solve: the outfit works only because it has become part of one character.  
Build: a cross-base transfer test.

Use the black leather Workshop comparison as the worked example. Define the look by garment shapes, material, accessories, palette, and attitude. Move those rules to a second base while preserving each subject's identity lock. Inspect both false sameness and excessive redesign.

Checkpoint: the look remains legible while the two characters remain distinct.

## Paid 7: Audit, Repair, and Package

Solve: accepted files, failures, and uncertainty are scattered and cannot guide the next production.  
Build: a continuity report and final production board.

Place the source record, identity lock, clean base, supported views, outfit assets, same-base test, cross-base test, failures, corrections, and uncertainty notes on one board or in one indexed source pack. Classify each failure as identity drift, wardrobe drift, style drift, scene drift, evidence gap, or tool artifact. Choose regeneration, editing, relabeling, or rejection.

Final checkpoint: another operator can reproduce the decision path without private knowledge. The result does not claim guaranteed model consistency.
