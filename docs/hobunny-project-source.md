# Hobunny Project Source

**Status:** Canonical project source  
**Project:** HobFarm Mannequin & Character  
**Character:** Hobunny  
**Primary website role:** Character dossier and first reusable example of the Mannequin & Character workflow  
**External publishing:** DeviantArt, social media, HobFarm galleries, video, and later product channels

## 1. Project definition

Hobunny is an adult rabbit-girl doll character, mascot, cartoon personality, and satirical character brand.

She begins with the familiar visual category of the Japanese usagimimi or kemonomimi rabbit girl and Western bunny-glam imagery: a predominantly human adult figure with rabbit ears, a puff tail, fashion styling, and an attractive presentation. That category supplies the baseline mannequin, but it does not yet create a meaningful character.

The Hobunny project demonstrates how a visually complete but meaning-neutral mannequin becomes a reusable character through stable identity, personality, purpose, satire, continuity, editions, and recurring public content.

The central project idea is:

> Hobunny is a character brand that parodies character brands.

She can appear in fashion images, cartoons, fake advertisements, avatars, posters, short videos, historical parodies, product concepts, mascot graphics, and Cute → Corrupted transformations without becoming a different character each time.

## 2. Role in the Mannequin & Character workflow

Hobunny is the first full template produced by HobFarm's Mannequin & Character method.

The method is documented once on the main Workshop page. Hobunny's page does not repeat the whole method. It shows the completed result and records the character's canon, modes, editions, public work, and continuity.

Use these terms consistently:

- **Reference category:** The existing visual and cultural lane being studied.
- **Mannequin:** An appearance-focused base used to test anatomy, face, style, species traits, wardrobe, and composition.
- **Character:** A mannequin with stable identity, personality, purpose, visual anchors, behavior, and continuity.
- **Edition:** A contextual interpretation of the same character through an era, scene, role, fashion system, media system, or commercial fantasy.
- **Content:** Public images, cartoons, clips, advertisements, sheets, and posts created from the character or an edition.
- **Premium layer:** Optional higher-value material developed after public recognition and interest exist.

The reusable sequence is:

**References → baseline mannequin → canonical character → editions → recurring public content → audience recognition → optional premium products**

## 3. Baseline mannequin

The baseline Hobunny mannequin is intentionally simple:

- Adult human doll anatomy
- Rabbit ears
- Puff tail
- Bunny-glam bodysuit, swimsuit, or similarly minimal fashion base
- Attractive pose
- Clean presentation
- Limited historical context
- Limited narrative information
- No fully developed personality or world

This stage is useful because it makes the distinction between appearance and character visible.

The baseline says, “rabbit girl.” It does not yet say why this specific rabbit girl exists, what she thinks, what she represents, or why an audience should recognize her again.

The swimsuit or bodysuit is not treated as meaningful characterization by itself. It is the neutral starting outfit used to establish the visual category and character construction.

## Hobunny facial key

Hobunny has an adult fashion-doll face with stylized proportions, but her head must remain compact, balanced, and structurally coherent.

- Compact oval-to-soft-heart head shape
- Moderately enlarged doll head, not a spherical, ballooned, or bulbous cranium
- Controlled forehead and temple width
- Smooth cheekbones with gently tapered lower cheeks
- Small, defined jaw and softly pointed chin
- Large glossy almond-shaped eyes, not round or googly
- Eyes positioned relatively close together, with the inner-corner gap approximately two-thirds to three-quarters of one eye width
- Narrow, clearly defined bridge between the eyes
- Irises centered consistently and directed toward the same focal point
- Slight upward lift at the outer eye corners
- Strong upper lash line with a lighter, cleaner lower lid
- Short, delicate nose centered directly beneath the eye gap
- Small glossy lips with a defined cupid’s bow
- Compact midface without compressing the nose, mouth, and chin together
- Adult editorial expression: poised, knowing, glamorous, and self-aware rather than vacant, babyish, or overly innocent
- Hair may create a large silhouette, but hairstyle volume must never be interpreted as skull volume

## 4. Style authority

`hobfarm-doll-style.md` remains the rendering authority for Hobunny character art.

Hobunny must use the locked HobFarm doll language:

- Oversized glossy eyes
- Large cranium and compact facial features
- Small glossy lips
- Doll proportions with long limbs and small joints
- Clean dark contour linework
- Smooth controlled skin gradients
- Material-specific gloss and highlights
- Deliberate makeup geometry
- One persistent liquid drip element
- No random painterly noise, sparkle soup, or uncontrolled micro-detail

Project-specific adaptations include:

- Rabbit ears and a visible tail or puff motif
- Adult fashion presentation
- Strong, repeatable silhouette
- Edition-specific wardrobe and styling
- Rabbit iconography integrated into clothing, products, graphics, or composition
- Clear continuity between normal and corrupted forms

Specific face key, hair construction, palette, ear asymmetry, tail design, and permanent identifier should be locked by the canonical Hobunny character sheet. Until approved, do not silently invent or treat temporary image details as permanent canon.

## 5. Core character identity

Hobunny is a self-aware commercial personality built for constant reinvention.

She enjoys fashion, glamour, novelty, spectacle, attention, and performance. She is not an outsider who simply condemns consumer culture. She participates in it, understands that it is manufactured, and still wants the deluxe version.

Her working central contradiction is:

> She knows she is a product, but she still wants to be the deluxe edition.

This gives Hobunny a stable point of view across different eras and media.

Her tone may move between charming, vain, theatrical, funny, aspirational, tired, cynical, and sincere. She should remain active inside the fantasy rather than functioning only as a narrator explaining the satire.

## 6. Satirical territory

Hobunny can parody and remix:

- Fashion-doll reinvention
- Japanese rabbit-girl character design
- Western bunny-glam imagery
- Mascot culture
- Branded cuteness
- Youth marketing
- Boutique identity
- Beauty culture
- Nightlife performance
- Vegas excess
- Rave and festival fashion
- Influencer self-branding
- Algorithmic identity
- Artificial authenticity
- Wellness marketing
- Commercial femininity
- Identity sold as an expandable product line
- The conversion of characters and personalities into premium content

The satire should emerge from the character, wardrobe, composition, product framing, setting, and contradiction. It should not depend on an explanatory essay attached to every image.

## 7. Permanent anchors

Every Hobunny version should preserve enough stable information to remain recognizable.

### Required identity anchors

- Adult rabbit-girl character
- HobFarm doll rendering language
- Rabbit ears
- Tail, puff, pom-pom, or equivalent rabbit-tail motif
- Large expressive eyes
- Strong fashion-doll silhouette
- Persistent liquid drip signature
- Self-aware commercial presentation
- Ability to move between glamour, comedy, and corruption

### Required conceptual anchors

- Identity as product
- Reinvention through editions
- Fashion as characterization
- Commercial polish with something sharper beneath it
- Recognition across multiple media and eras
- Public character development before premium exploitation

### Canon still to be locked

The following should remain open until a canonical character sheet is approved:

- Exact face key
- Primary eye color
- Hair color and permanent hair structure
- Ear shape and asymmetry
- Tail construction
- Core palette
- Permanent logo
- Exact drip placement and substance
- Default launch outfit
- Body silhouette family

Once approved, these become continuity requirements.

## 8. Character modes

### Doll Mode

The canonical adult rabbit-girl form.

Use Doll Mode for:

- Character portraits
- Fashion sheets
- Historical editions
- Posters
- Cartoons
- Fake advertisements
- Avatars
- Story scenes
- Social images
- Product concepts

Doll Mode preserves the main face, body, ears, tail, identity, and attitude while allowing era-specific styling.

### Mascot Mode

A simplified commercial interpretation of Hobunny.

Mascot Mode may appear as:

- Literal rabbit mascot
- Plush character
- Sticker
- Logo
- Brand spokesperson
- Product icon
- Cartoon sidekick
- Officially approved version of Doll Mode

Mascot Hobunny can appear beside Doll Hobunny. The two versions may cooperate, argue, contradict each other, or reveal the difference between a person and her brand-safe representation.

### Corrupted Mode

The same Hobunny character or edition after its fantasy, branding, environment, or identity begins to fail.

Corruption can involve:

- Faded glamour
- Blacklight grime
- Melted or broken branding
- Cosmetic breakdown
- Haunted locations
- Obsolete technology
- Failed luxury
- Abandoned resorts or retail spaces
- Emotional distortion
- Exhausted performance
- A mascot unable to maintain its approved expression

Corrupted Mode must preserve the edition's original identity. It should reveal the collapse of the fantasy, not replace Hobunny with an unrelated monster.

## 9. Edition system

An edition places Hobunny inside a specific era, scene, industry, role, fashion system, media system, or commercial fantasy.

An edition is not merely a costume change.

Each edition should define:

- **Title**
- **Status**
- **Era or cultural setting**
- **Source references**
- **Promised fantasy**
- **Hobunny's role**
- **Fashion vocabulary**
- **Hair and makeup vocabulary**
- **Palette and materials**
- **Composition language**
- **Props and environment**
- **Commercial products, slogans, or playsets**
- **Satirical contradiction**
- **Mascot translation**
- **Corrupted outcome**
- **Planned public outputs**
- **Continuity notes**

A successful edition adds meaning to the baseline mannequin while preserving the canonical character.

## 10. Hobunny '66

**Hobunny '66** is the first authored edition and the initial demonstration of the edition workflow.

### Edition thesis

Hobunny '66 takes the meaning-neutral rabbit-girl mannequin and places her inside a mid-1960s editorial fantasy built around youth, novelty, boutique fashion, beauty marketing, and the repeated commercial promise of “the new.”

The satire is based on HobFarm's interpretation of the broader cultural moment:

- Youth recognized as a major market
- Fashion media packaging modern identity
- Beauty and clothing selling a controlled form of novelty
- Boutique taste presented as individuality
- A polished image of freedom produced through commercial systems

Do not attribute this thesis directly to the inaccessible Vogue article titled “The New Romantics” unless the full article is later obtained and supports that claim.

### Hobunny's role

Possible roles include:

- Boutique fashion doll
- Editorial model
- Beauty mascot
- Department-store personality
- Aspirational young romantic
- Spokescharacter for the newest version of modern femininity

### Visual direction

Possible vocabulary includes:

- Lace collars
- Bows
- Structured coats
- Tights
- Graphic accessories
- Controlled editorial posing
- Clean magazine layouts
- Beauty-counter and boutique settings
- Period-aware typography and packaging

The outfit and composition must communicate the edition's thesis. Do not reduce Hobunny '66 to a generic retro costume.

### Commercial layer

## 12. Distribution model

The website is the canonical foundation.

The Hobunny character dossier should answer:

- What is Hobunny?
- What remains consistent?
- What is the character for?
- What modes and editions exist?
- Where can someone find public Hobunny work?

DeviantArt, social media, HobFarm galleries, and video channels distribute individual pieces.

A viewer may discover one Hobunny image elsewhere and follow it back to the website for the complete character definition.

Do not make the website dependent on live social feeds or unstable platform embeds.

## 13. Premium strategy

Premium content is downstream of character recognition.

The intended sequence is:

1. Establish the baseline character publicly.
2. Release multiple meaningful editions.
3. Build recurring visual and personality-based content.
4. Create recognition and interest.
5. Test products, collector material, exclusives, and premium variants.
6. Price premium work based on character interest and production value rather than treating it as disposable low-cost output.

The adult or premium version of Hobunny becomes more valuable when it is an alternate version of an established character rather than the first and only thing the audience sees.

Do not define Hobunny primarily as a premium-content character. Premium work is one later publishing lane.

## 14. Content creation workflow

For every new Hobunny concept:

1. **Identify the request.** Image, cartoon, poster, edition, clip, product, social post, or Workshop material.
2. **Choose the mode.** Doll, Mascot, Corrupted, or a deliberate combination.
3. **Choose the edition.** Existing edition, baseline mannequin, or proposed new edition.
4. **Define the purpose.** What does this piece add to the character?
5. **Define the cultural fantasy or target.** What is being sold, performed, or parodied?
6. **Define the contradiction.** What is the gap between the promise and reality?
7. **Apply canon.** Preserve approved face, silhouette, ears, tail, palette, drip, personality, and continuity.
8. **Design the composition.** Pose, expression, wardrobe, setting, props, framing, and graphic treatment.
9. **Select the output.** Single, sheet, poster, comic, ad, clip, avatar, product concept, or transformation.
10. **Create derivatives only when useful.** Do not force every concept into every medium.
11. **Publish with context.** Use a concise caption and link back to the canonical Hobunny page where appropriate.
12. **Record continuity.** Save edition, outfit, palette, prompt, output, and status.

## 16. Naming conventions

Use:

- **Hobunny** for the canonical character.
- **Hobunny '66** for the first historical edition.
- **Hobunny [Edition Name]** or **Hobunny '[Year]** for later editions.
- **Doll Mode**, **Mascot Mode**, and **Corrupted Mode** for operating forms.
- **Baseline Hobunny Mannequin** for the meaning-neutral starting design.

Do not describe every unrelated rabbit-girl image as Hobunny. It becomes Hobunny only when the canonical identity and project logic are applied.

## 17. Creative boundaries

Do not reduce Hobunny to:

- Generic bunny-girl art
- A random swimsuit character
- A different face in every image
- A single black bunny-suit costume
- Disconnected fashion experiments
- A direct copy of Barbie or an existing Japanese character
- A generic kawaii mascot
- A random horror transformation
- A premium-content gimmick without public character development
- Lore so heavy that simple images and jokes become difficult to produce

Do not:

- Invent permanent canon without approval
- Treat reference art as publishable Hobunny artwork
- Copy online reference images into products or website art
- Fabricate historical claims
- Attribute an interpretation to an unread source
- Add visual details that conflict with the locked doll style
- Make every piece explain the entire project

## 18. Success criteria

The project is working when:

- Hobunny is recognizable across outfits, eras, and media
- The baseline mannequin and authored editions are visibly different in meaning
- A single image works without a long explanation
- The website answers “What is Hobunny?”
- The Workshop answers “How did a bunny-girl mannequin become this character?”
- Hobunny has a stable voice and visual identity
- New editions can be created without rebuilding the character
- Public content creates interest in the character
- Premium work can later build on that interest
- The same workflow can be reused for future HobFarm characters
