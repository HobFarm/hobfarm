## 1. Project definition

Hobunny is a HobFarm character-development project built around an adult
rabbit-girl fashion doll, mascot, cartoon personality, and satirical character
brand.

The project combines fashion-doll logic, Japanese usagimimi and kemonomimi
character design, bunny-glam imagery, commercial mascot culture, historical
fashion, nightlife aesthetics, social-media performance, and Cute → Corrupted
transformation.

Hobunny is not only one illustration or character design. She is a reusable
character property with a defined identity, visual system, personality, world,
satire engine, and production structure.

The central idea is:

**Hobunny is a character brand that parodies character brands.**

She can appear in cartoons, fashion images, fake advertisements, short videos,
posters, avatars, collectible designs, historical parodies, social posts, and
corrupted transformations while remaining recognizably the same character.

## 2. Purpose of the project

Two connected purposes.

### Public character purpose

Hobunny exists as an active HobFarm character whose content can appear across
HobFarm, social media, DeviantArt, short-form video, posters and fake
advertisements, cartoons and comic panels, character sheets, digital products,
and experimental image and animation workflows.

Someone may first encounter Hobunny through a single image, cartoon, clip, or
fake advertisement. The website provides the permanent foundation that explains
what Hobunny is.

### Workshop purpose

Hobunny also serves as a documented character-development case study.

Her Workshop material demonstrates that character creation involves more than
designing a face, outfit, or visual style. A durable character also needs a
purpose, a recognizable identity, a point of view, a visual language, a
personality, a world or cultural context, recurring themes, a content engine,
rules for variation, and a relationship between individual works and the larger
character property.

The Hobunny project continues the ideas introduced on the Mannequin and
Character page. That page explains the movement from a neutral visual base
toward a designed character. Hobunny demonstrates what happens after that
initial design stage, when the character gains meaning, behavior, context,
history, and a reason to exist.

## 3. Role within the HobFarm website

Hobunny should exist in two connected website contexts.

### Character page

The public introduction. It answers: Who is Hobunny? What does she look like?
What kind of character is she? What does she parody? What kinds of content does
she appear in? Where can someone see more?

This page should be understandable to someone arriving from a social post with
no prior knowledge of HobFarm or the Workshop. It should introduce the character
clearly without requiring the visitor to read the complete development history.

### Workshop section

Documents how Hobunny was developed: how the original idea changed, how the
visual concept became a character concept, how the satire was defined, how
permanent traits were separated from temporary styles, how character modes and
historical editions work, how the mascot, doll, cartoon, avatar, and corrupted
forms relate, how the character can support multiple types of content, and how
the same development method can be used for other characters.

The Workshop material is not merely behind-the-scenes commentary. It is part of
HobFarm's character-design methodology.

## 4. Relationship to the Mannequin and Character system

The Mannequin and Character page establishes a progression:

```text
mannequin -> visual design -> character
```

A mannequin provides a base for testing body shape, face design, hair, wardrobe,
accessories, color, style combinations, pose, and rendering systems.

A visually complete figure is not automatically a fully developed character.

The Hobunny project continues the progression:

```text
character design -> character identity -> character system -> character property
```

Hobunny demonstrates how a design becomes reusable by adding a stable identity, a
central contradiction, a personality and voice, a cultural role, a satire target,
recurring motifs, transformation logic, a set of content formats, and a world
that can expand around the character.

Hobunny should therefore be presented as a practical case study in moving beyond
appearance.

## 5. Core character concept

Hobunny is an adult rabbit-girl fashion character, lifestyle mascot, and
professional rebrand.

Every era gives her a new wardrobe, occupation, fantasy, product line, social
role, and version of happiness to sell. She can become a beach-party starlet,
resort hostess, disco celebrity, aerobics entrepreneur, mall icon, rave creature,
Vegas personality, wellness founder, digital avatar, influencer, ghost, mascot,
or failed commercial product.

Her appearance changes, but the underlying character remains consistent.

Hobunny genuinely enjoys fashion, glamour, novelty, spectacle, attention, and
reinvention. She is not an outsider looking down on popular culture. She
participates in it, understands how artificial it is, and keeps performing
anyway.

Her central contradiction is:

**She knows she is a product, but she still wants to be the deluxe edition.**

This contradiction gives the character a stable point of view across different
settings and time periods.

## 6. Satirical territory

### Fashion-doll culture

Expandable identity logic: new occupations, outfits, vehicles, homes and
playsets, historical editions, fantasy lifestyles, endless commercial
reinvention. The satire focuses on the idea that identity can be packaged,
accessorized, and purchased.

### Usagimimi and kemonomimi character culture

The recognizable structure of a human rabbit character: rabbit ears, rabbit tail,
human adult anatomy, bunny-coded gestures and accessories, cute animal traits
incorporated into fashion design. This gives Hobunny a flexible silhouette that
can move between fashion illustration, anime-influenced design, cartoons,
avatars, mascots, and nightlife imagery.

### Bunny-glam culture

Western bunny-girl imagery: nightlife, cabaret, Vegas, hostess glamour, pin-up
imagery, performance femininity, adult fashion, branded fantasy. This material
can be used directly, historically, comedically, or critically.

### Commercial mascot culture

Hobunny can be simplified into a cute rabbit mascot, plush toy, sticker, logo,
product icon, or brand-safe cartoon. This creates a second layer of satire: the
commercial mascot is the polished, simplified, officially approved version of the
more complicated character.

### Historical lifestyle fantasy

Different time periods sold different versions of freedom, glamour, success,
youth, beauty, and happiness. Hobunny can inhabit those fantasies and expose
their assumptions through fashion, cartoons, fake products, and Cute → Corrupted
transformations.

### Contemporary identity culture

Influencer self-branding, algorithmic personality, wellness marketing, content
production, subscription identity, VIP access, artificial authenticity, creator
exhaustion, fashion as personal branding, and the transformation of personality
into a product line.

## 7. Permanent character anchors

Hobunny must remain recognizable even when her time period, wardrobe, medium, and
rendering style change.

**Character identity:** adult rabbit-girl character; fashion-doll and mascot
logic; self-aware commercial personality; attraction to spectacle and
reinvention; a polished surface with a sharper underlying viewpoint.

**Physical anchors:** recognizable rabbit ears; a tail, puff, pom-pom, or
circular rabbit-tail motif; large expressive eyes; strong, readable silhouette;
consistent facial construction; a recognizable hair structure or recurring hair
motif; adult proportions; theatrical expression and posing.

**Graphic anchors:** rabbit-face iconography; heart-nose or bunny-head symbols;
circular puff shapes; fake logos and product badges; glossy commercial
presentation; packaging and advertisement language; a recurring Hobunny wordmark
or emblem.

**Behavioral anchors:** treats disasters as rebranding opportunities; speaks
comfortably in promotional language; is difficult to embarrass permanently;
understands that appearances are constructed; wants the fantasy even after seeing
the machinery behind it; can move between sincerity, vanity, charm, cynicism, and
absurd optimism.

## 8. Character modes

### Doll Mode

The canonical character form. The adult human rabbit-girl used for character
portraits, fashion sheets, historical editions, posters, cartoons, fake
advertisements, avatars, story scenes, social content, and product designs.

Doll Mode preserves Hobunny's main face, body, ears, tail, personality, and
graphic identity while allowing wardrobe, setting, hairstyle variation, and
historical redesign.

### Mascot Mode

A simplified commercial version. May appear as a literal rabbit, plush mascot,
sticker character, logo, brand spokesperson, cartoon sidekick, product icon, a
toy based on Doll Mode Hobunny, or an officially approved version of the
character.

Mascot Hobunny can exist beside Doll Hobunny. The two versions can cooperate,
compete, contradict each other, or reveal different sides of the character.
Mascot Mode may behave more politely, follow brand rules, or attempt to hide what
Doll Mode is actually doing.

### Corrupted Mode

What happens when a Hobunny fantasy, product, identity, or era begins to fail.
Abandoned glamour, blacklight grime, melted branding, cosmetic breakdown, haunted
environments, emotional distortion, broken commercial language, synthetic excess,
obsolete technology, failed luxury, decaying resorts, exhausted performance, a
mascot that can no longer maintain its approved expression.

Corrupted Mode must preserve the original character and edition. It should reveal
the collapse of the same fantasy, not replace Hobunny with an unrelated monster.

## 9. Hobunny Editions

Historical and cultural variations organized as Editions. An edition places the
same character inside a specific period, lifestyle fantasy, subculture, industry,
or commercial environment.

Each edition defines:

- **Era or cultural setting.** The time period, movement, industry, scene, or
  social environment being explored.
- **Promised fantasy.** What the era claimed to offer: permanent youth, leisure,
  romance, freedom, glamour, career success, technological optimism, social
  status, authenticity, community, reinvention.
- **Hobunny role.** Starlet, hostess, performer, career doll, resort mascot, club
  personality, raver, influencer, founder, avatar, spokescharacter, ghost of a
  failed product.
- **Fashion and visual system.** Wardrobe, hair, makeup, accessories, palette,
  graphic design, props, architecture, vehicles, packaging, media format.
- **Commercial layer.** Products, advertisements, playsets, slogans, memberships,
  services, or lifestyle packages.
- **Contradiction.** The difference between what the edition promises and what it
  actually contains.
- **Corrupted outcome.** The Cute → Corrupted or fantasy → collapse version.

## 10. Initial edition directions

- **Beach Party Hobunny.** 1950s and 1960s beach-party movies, teenage leisure,
  surf fashion, novelty music, resort fantasy, manufactured innocence.
- **Resort Disco Hobunny.** 1970s leisure and nightlife: resort entertainment,
  orange interiors, dancing, cocktails, celebrity culture, synthetic liberation.
  Its corrupted form may haunt the abandoned remains of the resort.
- **Career Hobunny.** 1980s fashion-doll fantasy: endless occupations, products,
  outfits, obligations, and accessories, but no visible time to live.
- **Mall Hobunny.** Late-1980s or 1990s mall culture, beauty counters, food
  courts, teen retail, promotional events, the mall as a complete social world.
- **Rave Hobunny.** Rave, festival, scene, cyber, kandi, club, and nightlife
  fashion. The fantasy centers on transformation, community, self-expression, and
  temporary escape. The corrupted form reveals exhaustion, synthetic overload,
  status competition, and commercialized rebellion.
- **Vegas Hobunny.** Casino fantasy, bottle service, performers, VIP culture,
  promotional models, luxury excess, fake exclusivity, and the labor hidden
  behind spectacle.
- **Creator Hobunny.** Contemporary: fashion, wellness, vulnerability,
  aspiration, adult glamour, education, access, and personality packaged into a
  constantly updated personal brand.

## 11. Character voice

Concise, self-aware, promotional, glamorous, and slightly cynical. She often
describes obvious failure using the language of product launches, personal
growth, wellness, fashion, or brand management.

- "She can be anything. Terms and conditions apply."
- "My personality is available in six colorways."
- "It's not a breakdown. It's a brand refresh."
- "New decade. New dream. Same rabbit."
- "Authenticity sold separately."
- "This lifestyle is no longer supported."
- "I'm entering my discontinued era."
- "The fantasy is real. The furniture is cardboard."
- "Every version is the real me."
- "Please remain aspirational during the emergency."

These lines establish tone but should not become mandatory catchphrases.

## 12. Content system

**Discovery content.** Short pieces designed to introduce Hobunny visually:
single images, posters, fashion portraits, short clips, fake advertisements,
transformation videos, one-panel cartoons, social captions, DeviantArt uploads.
These should work independently and direct interested viewers back to the
website.

**Character content.** Pieces that build personality: comic strips, avatar
monologues, reactions, advice columns, promotional videos, fake interviews, brand
statements, product demonstrations, recurring jokes, dialogue between Doll and
Mascot Hobunny.

**Edition content.** Material tied to a particular historical or cultural
version: character sheets, era-specific posters, fake products, playsets,
vehicles, advertisements, short stories, Cute → Corrupted pairs, before and after
sequences, supporting characters and environments.

**Workshop content.** Material that explains the development process: early
concept, reference categories, visual experiments, rejected directions, character
anchors, personality development, mode development, edition design, worldbuilding,
prompt and production tests, continuity problems, final decisions, lessons that
apply to other characters.

## 15. Worldbuilding approach

Hobunny does not need a single conventional fictional universe at the beginning.
Her world can grow through commercial fragments: advertisements, packages,
catalogs, resorts, clubs, stores, television appearances, product launches, doll
boxes, vehicles, playsets, mascot costumes, failed attractions, abandoned
locations, promotional events, fake social accounts.

Continuity should be flexible enough to support multiple eras but strong enough
that recurring places, brands, objects, and supporting characters can return.

Each edition may be treated as a historical incarnation, a manufactured reboot, a
fictional product line, a performance role, an alternate continuity, a memory or
fantasy, or a version Hobunny insists was always canonical. The ambiguity can
become part of the joke.

## 16. Production framework

**Concept record:** title, edition, mode, era or cultural context, central
fantasy, Hobunny role, satire target, visual anchors, wardrobe, palette, props,
environment, commercial product or slogan, contradiction, corrupted form, intended
output, continuity notes.

**Possible outputs:** portrait, character sheet, poster, fake advertisement,
single-panel cartoon, three-panel comic, sticker, social image, short video,
avatar segment, Cute → Corrupted pair, product mockup, Workshop process entry,
DeviantArt product or exclusive.

Not every concept needs every output.

## 17. Initial foundation assets

Canonical Doll Mode character sheet; Mascot Mode translation; Corrupted Mode test;
core expression sheet; ear and tail design; hair and facial continuity rules;
primary Hobunny logo or emblem; core palette; character voice notes; public
character description; Workshop introduction; first complete Hobunny Edition; one
cartoon demonstrating personality; one fake advertisement demonstrating satire;
one Cute → Corrupted transformation; website hero image; social discovery image
linking back to the character page.

## 18. Project boundaries

Hobunny should not become a generic rabbit-girl image collection; a different
character in every outfit; a direct copy of Barbie or another existing character
property; a project limited to nightclub bunny imagery; a generic kawaii mascot; a
random horror transformation system; a collection of disconnected aesthetics; a
corporate-safe motivational mascot; a lore-heavy universe that prevents simple
cartoons and visual jokes; or a Workshop project with no active public character
output.

The website documentation and public content should support each other. The
Workshop gives the character depth and permanence. The public content gives the
Workshop project a visible result.

## 19. Success criteria

- A viewer can recognize Hobunny across different eras and styles
- A single image can work without a full explanation
- The website can answer "What is Hobunny?"
- The Workshop can answer "How was Hobunny developed?"
- The character has a recognizable voice
- New editions can be created without rebuilding the concept
- Cute → Corrupted transformations reveal something about the original fantasy
- Social and DeviantArt content direct interested viewers toward the permanent
  character foundation
- Hobunny demonstrates the difference between visual design and full character
  development
- The system produces usable cartoons, images, videos, fake products, and stories
  rather than remaining an abstract design exercise

## 20. Working project statement

Hobunny is a continuing HobFarm character-development project about turning a
visual design into a reusable character property. She is an adult rabbit-girl
fashion doll, mascot, cartoon personality, and professional rebrand who moves
through historical fantasies, nightlife cultures, commercial identities, and
modern self-promotion. Her public content appears across images, cartoons,
videos, fake advertisements, and social platforms. Her permanent character
definition and development process live in the HobFarm Workshop, where the
project demonstrates how appearance, personality, purpose, satire, worldbuilding,
continuity, and production systems combine to create a character that can support
more than one picture.
