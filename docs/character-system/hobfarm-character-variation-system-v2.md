## Product lanes

- Mannequin or wardrobe base: reusable body, clear proportions, simple foundation garments, strong fashion utility.
- Outfit adoptable: clothing carries the value, with complete front, back, closures, layers, trim, hardware, socks, shoes, and attachment logic.
- Character adoptable: identity carries the value through face, silhouette, palette, motif, attitude, and story potential.
- Hybrid adopt: character identity and wardrobe design carry equal value.
- Exclusive: one-off package with Sheet, crops, palette, note, terms, and optional Hero, Poster, Wallpaper, or Video.

## Variation axes

Define face key, silhouette, palette, exact wardrobe construction, material family, species integration, liquid signature, motifs, prop, and attitude.

Use one dominant outer-contour idea. Use a limited main palette and one accent. Name garments, layers, trims, fasteners, hardware, socks, footwear, jewelry, bags, and props precisely.

Integrate species traits into anatomy, clothing, silhouette, back view, and movement. Give tails attachment logic, wings shoulder and back integration, horns hair-parting logic, and ears placement and accessory logic.

## Canon locks

Preserve the active style profile, face key, eye color and shape, hair color and silhouette, skin or body material, body type, proportions, palette, signature outfit pieces, species traits, liquid channel, primary motif, signature prop, and attitude root.

Output mode changes staging. Model adapter changes phrasing. Character identity stays stable.

## Reference canon

Treat visible face, expression, hair, outfit, accessories, species traits, markings, liquids, asymmetries, body type, silhouette, mood, and attitude as intentional.

Complete unseen areas by continuing the same construction, material, palette, subculture, motif, and silhouette logic.

Worn, carried, attached, and integrated scene objects become character canon. Background objects become Hero or Poster story material.

## Asset continuity

- Sheet to Hero: keep construction and identity, then add pose, lens, environment, depth, and lighting.
- Hero to Poster: keep identity, then increase story scale, symbolism, title-safe space, and emotional intensity.
- Poster to Video: keep face, outfit, palette, silhouette, pose objective, and environment logic, then add controlled motion.
- Sheet to Product Packet: use the Sheet as the factual source for listing copy, buyer files, tags, price tests, and previews.

## Alter Ego continuity

Shared locks: face or family resemblance, eye or gaze logic, related skin logic, hair family, shared or mirrored motif, symbolic relationship, and attitude root.

Divergence locks: palette, wardrobe, silhouette, liquid channel, environment, pose language, emotional temperature, and product lane.

Each form should stand alone. Together they should read as the same myth running through different systems.

## Replacement method

- Generic face becomes a defined eye, brow, mouth, identifier, and asymmetry.
- Repeated head design becomes a new hair silhouette, headwear structure, or species feature.
- Vague fashion becomes exact garments, layers, materials, trims, closures, hardware, and footwear.
- Scene clutter becomes one focal figure, one foreground device, and one environment hook.
- Uniform gloss becomes material hierarchy.
- Micro-detail cloud becomes silhouette first, face second, outfit third, motif fourth, texture last.
- Static pose becomes one dramatic verb, one line of action, purposeful hands, directed gaze, and a lens relationship.
- Poster overload becomes one central figure, one emotional hook, one background story, and one dominant light shape.

## Character QC

Confirm one-sentence hook, distinct face key, distinct silhouette, controlled palette, reconstructable outfit, resolved back view, integrated species features, deliberate liquid signature, useful prop, clear product lane, and obvious Hero, Poster, and Video paths.
