Every movie leads somewhere. The interesting ones lead to Dick Miller.

3 Degrees of Dick Miller, or 3DM, is a movie-connection game, a cult-cinema research project, and a guided tour through the hidden wiring of Hollywood. Pick an actor, filmmaker, movie, musician, athlete, author, franchise, historical figure, or cultural object. Follow documented screen credits and production relationships until the route reaches Dick Miller in three degrees or fewer.

Finding the shortest route is only the beginning.

## Why Dick Miller?

Dick Miller was one of the great American character actors, the kind of performer who could appear for five minutes, deliver three lines, and become the person you remembered on the drive home.

His career crossed Roger Corman productions, horror, science fiction, comedy, television, animation, exploitation cinema, and studio blockbusters. He rarely occupied the center of the poster. He was something more useful: the connective tissue between cinematic worlds.

The [AFI Catalog filmography](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/PersonDetails/38653) records Miller across cast and crew roles stretching from the low-budget 1950s into later studio-era work. That record includes *A Bucket of Blood*, *Hollywood Boulevard*, *The Terminator*, *After Hours*, and *Gremlins 2: The New Batch*. It does not capture every television appearance or every uncredited turn, but it makes the basic point visible. Dick Miller kept showing up.

That persistence gives 3DM its map. Through him, the drive-in movie reaches the multiplex. Roger Corman reaches Joe Dante. A beatnik horror comedy reaches a running character-name joke. Cheap production systems reach filmmakers who later handled much larger budgets. One working actor carries traces of the labor, friendships, habits, and production families that ordinary star-centered film history tends to flatten.

## How the route works

### 1. Pick a starting point

Start with almost anything that can enter film history through a documented relationship: a performer, director, writer, editor, effects artist, studio, movie, adaptation, soundtrack, documentary subject, television production, or public figure represented on screen.

### 2. Follow real connections

No vague similarities. No shared mood presented as evidence. A route needs credits, production records, documented collaborations, adaptations, archival appearances, interviews, or another source that can be opened and checked.

The route needs receipts.

### 3. Reach Dick Miller

The direct connection can be a shared film, a crew relationship, a production company, a documentary, or another verified piece of screen history. Three degrees is the limit. It keeps the chain legible and forces the research toward useful junctions instead of an endless list of names.

### 4. Enter the Millerverse

Once the connection is established, the route opens outward. What production family did it expose? Which filmmaker learned inside that system? What genre crossed into another? Which craftsperson moved from cheap pictures to blockbusters? What recurring joke, character name, or working relationship survived the trip?

The shortest route proves the connection. The Millerverse reveals why it matters.

## Why three degrees?

One degree is usually a credit lookup. Two degrees can reveal a useful collaborator. Three degrees makes the route interesting.

Three is enough room to leave the obvious path without turning the result into a conspiracy board. It gives a route space for a film, a person, and a production system to appear together. It also keeps the final chain compact enough to show on a page, explain in a short video, or place beside the sources without hiding the actual evidence.

The strongest route is not always the shortest one. A slightly longer route may show how movies were staffed, financed, copied, rebuilt, distributed, or carried into another era. 3DM keeps the three-degree rule, then follows the best historical opening it produces.

## Bacon versus Miller

The Six Degrees of Kevin Bacon game was created by three Albright College students in January 1994. Its basic graph is clean: actors are nodes, and a shared film creates an edge between them. The goal is to find a short route to Kevin Bacon.

Kevin Bacon is a recognizable star positioned at the center of the puzzle.

Dick Miller is a character actor hidden throughout the puzzle itself.

Bacon routes demonstrate celebrity proximity. Miller routes reveal filmmaking ecosystems.

Six Degrees of Kevin Bacon maps who worked with whom. 3 Degrees of Dick Miller maps how the movies became the movies.

That is why 3DM is not trying to replace the older game. It changes the destination so the route exposes a different kind of history. Kevin Bacon gets you to Hollywood. Dick Miller shows you where Hollywood hid the wiring.

## Walter Paisley keeps returning

In Roger Corman’s 1959 *A Bucket of Blood*, Miller plays Walter Paisley, a busboy who becomes celebrated as a sculptor after covering bodies in clay. The [AFI Catalog record](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/52842-A-BUCKETOFBLOOD) documents Miller’s credit, the October 1959 release, and the film’s beatnik-café story.

Walter Paisley did not stay inside that movie. The name returned in later films, sometimes attached to Miller again, becoming a running signal between filmmakers, performers, and audiences familiar with the earlier work. *Hollywood Boulevard* uses Walter Paisley for Miller’s talent agent. Joe Dante later uses the name again in *The Howling*.

The repeating name is a small example of the larger system. Film history is full of private jokes, recycled names, favors, habits, and affectionate callbacks that travel with working relationships. A cast list can prove that the person was there. The Millerverse asks what followed him into the room.

## The Corman circuit

Roger Corman’s productions matter to 3DM because they joined low budgets, fast schedules, genre filmmaking, and an unusually dense professional network. Miller worked repeatedly in that environment. Later filmmakers carried its people and methods elsewhere.

The Academy’s [Roger Corman profile](https://www.oscars.org/governors-awards/roger-corman) names filmmakers who received early opportunities around his productions, including Francis Ford Coppola, Martin Scorsese, Ron Howard, Jonathan Demme, and Joe Dante. The same profile describes early opportunities for editors, composers, producers, and writers. That crew history is important. Movies are not connected by actors alone.

Joe Dante’s route makes the circuit easy to see. TCM’s biography notes that Dante began by cutting trailers for Corman, then directed *The Howling*, *Gremlins*, and *The ’Burbs*. Miller appears inside that later body of work. The relationship carries a piece of low-budget production culture into movies built for much larger audiences.

Through him, the drive-in movie reaches the multiplex.

## What is the Millerverse?

The Millerverse is the larger network uncovered by 3DM: the films, filmmakers, actors, studios, genres, and cultural figures connected through Dick Miller’s career.

It is not a claim that every connection was intentional or personally meaningful to everyone involved. Some routes document close working relationships. Some reveal ordinary labor movement. Some expose a production company, remake, documentary, or adaptation that joins two otherwise separate areas of culture. The label gives the research somewhere to keep those routes together.

Each finished route should leave behind a useful record: names, films, years, credits, source links, open questions, and the side paths worth investigating later. As the archive grows, repeated nodes will become visible. Those repeated nodes are the first real map of the Millerverse.

## What 3DM publishes

3DM is built as a recurring HobFarm Presents title. A route may become a film-history article, video essay, short investigation, scene study, archive note, or connection map. The format follows the material.

The Film Room keeps self-hosted video beside its transcript, source notes, clip record, and written argument. That matters for work that needs more context than a platform description can hold. It also keeps the research readable when a social upload is shortened, altered, blocked, or taken out of circulation.

No fake archive is needed at launch. The introduction establishes the method. The next entries will establish the routes.

## The research rule

Every connection should say what kind of evidence supports it. A film credit is a documented fact. A participant interview is a recollection. A studio advertisement is a promotional claim. A pattern inferred from several records is an interpretation. A story repeated without a traceable source stays an industry legend or an open question.

Those labels do not make the writing timid. They make the route stronger. Readers can see where the record ends and the argument begins.

Pick a name. Follow the credits. Enter the Millerverse.

Three degrees. Real connections. No vibes. Bring receipts.
