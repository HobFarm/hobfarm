This Before & After piece places the same character in two different systems of presentation. On the left, she belongs to 1926: pearls, gloves, beadwork, vintage posture, and the composed elegance of a public world built around restraint and ornament. On the right, she becomes a modern visual persona: phone in hand, ring light glow, iced coffee, styled confidence, and the strange performance of looking effortless in a space designed to be photographed.

The character underneath stays the same. Her face, proportions, eye shape, and core identity remain consistent across both sides. The transformation happens through the costume of the era: what she wears, how she stands, what props matter, what kind of room or street surrounds her, and what each time period expects from a woman being looked at.

The animated version turns the comparison into a short loop. The 1926 version blinks, shifts her posture, and lets the beadwork and pearls catch the light. The modern version adjusts her selfie pose, smiles slightly, and lets the ring light shimmer behind her. For a few seconds, both versions feel alive, like two performances of the same person separated by a century.
