This course does not end with a correct set of opinions.

It ends with a maintenance document.

You now have methods for defining a chatbot's job, opening receipts, rebuilding answers from sources, writing creative source files, detecting scope drift, routing work, and tracing media chains. The methods become yours when you decide where they apply, how much checking each decision deserves, and what you refuse to outsource.

An independent mind is maintained, not completed.

## Historical lineage, kept proportional

In a 2001 interview, Noam Chomsky described the purpose of his work with Edward S. Herman as encouraging what might be called a course in intellectual self-defense: helping people resist efforts that turn them into passive objects instead of agents who control their own fate.

Normand Baillargeon later published *A Short Course in Intellectual Self-Defense*, a broad toolkit covering logic, language, numbers, science, and media. This HobFarm course works in a narrower workshop: AI-assisted research, AI-assisted creation, mixed-origin media, scope control, and personal protocols.

The lineage is not a request to copy an authority. The point is to remain an active agent. Your protocol should disagree with this course wherever your work proves a better rule.

## Start with your pause signals

Write the feelings and situations that make you least reliable. Anger, fear, flattery, urgency, tribal recognition, a bargain that must be claimed now, and a result that confirms what you already believe can all shorten the route between exposure and action.

A pause signal is not proof that the item is false. It is a reason to slow the handoff.

Examples:

- I want to share before opening the link.
- The claim makes an opponent look perfectly stupid.
- The result flatters my taste or expertise.
- A confident answer lets me avoid an uncomfortable unknown.
- The tool has produced so much work that rejecting it feels wasteful.

## Write source thresholds by consequence

Not every claim needs the same investigation.

Define what requires an original source, what needs multiple independent sources, and what a qualified secondary source can carry. Raise the threshold for medical, legal, financial, safety, reputational, and irreversible decisions.

Also write a low-stakes rule. You should be able to explore, speculate, and enjoy fiction without turning every afternoon into a deposition.

## Keep the gap rules visible

Finish these sentences:

- Fact gaps require me to ______.
- Creative gaps may be invented when ______.
- Decision gaps require approval from ______.

Your answers may vary by project. A fictional name can be open in one brief and locked canon in another. The category depends on the assignment.

## Reserve decisions deliberately

List what you do not outsource. This may include belief, final argument, public accusation, canon, pricing, purchase, publication, upload, or the decision to stop.

Then list what you are happy to delegate: source discovery, terminology, comparison tables, translation drafts, cleanup, variations, file organization, or first-pass diagrams.

Delegation is not surrender when the boundary is visible.

## Write your scope-drift response

Choose a default action when a tool expands the job:

1. record the drift;
2. separate useful artifacts from unauthorized decisions;
3. keep, salvage, iterate, or dump;
4. restart the next job with a clean handoff when correction debt rises;
5. approve canon only in the source file or record you control.

The rule prevents an organized response from becoming official merely because it arrived first.

## State uncertainty in your own voice

Write phrases you will actually use:

- “The source confirms the date, not the motive.”
- “Possible, not proven.”
- “I have one participant's account and no independent record.”
- “The image history is visible; the claim in the caption remains unsupported.”
- “I do not know enough to share this.”

Uncertainty language should be plain. You are recording a limit, not performing humility.

## Define the sharing and stopping rules

Before sharing, decide what you check: the original, the date, the caption, the source identity, the strongest contrary evidence, or the consequence of being wrong.

Then define when you stop investigating. A good stopping rule can be:

- the important claim is supported enough for the decision at hand;
- remaining uncertainty is clear;
- a better source is unavailable and the limit is recorded;
- the issue does not deserve more attention;
- the investigation is feeding emotion rather than improving the decision.

Stopping is part of intellectual self-defense. So is changing your mind.

## Exercise: keep, edit, reject

Review the field rules from every lesson:

1. The answer box is an interface. It is not the source.
2. Define the job before judging the answer.
3. A citation is a route to evidence, not evidence by itself.
4. Use AI to widen the investigation before allowing it to close the question.
5. Give the machine a style source, not a vibe cloud.
6. Organization is not obedience.
7. Output is not a workflow.
8. The robot gets inspected. The human caption does too.
9. The method belongs to the person using it.

Mark each rule keep, edit, or reject. Rewrite the edited rules in language you would remember under pressure.
