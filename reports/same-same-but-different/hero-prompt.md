# Hero generation brief: One Machine, Many Storefronts

## Production intent

Create an original wide editorial illustration for the HobFarm article **Same Same, But Different**.

The image explains that many AI platforms are different storefronts connected to overlapping models, APIs, infrastructure, moderation systems, credit schemes, and workflow machinery.

## Prompt

A wide satirical magazine cutaway illustration of a bizarre commercial machine called creative AI, viewed straight on like an old technical manual mixed with 1950s MAD Magazine, 1970s National Lampoon, EC Comics machinery, newsprint halftone, hand-inked editorial cartooning, and a repair-shop diagram.

Above ground is a row of generic storefront booths with no real company logos. Each booth offers a different category through simple signs and objects: IMAGE, VIDEO, CHAT, VOICE, CANVAS, API, UNLIMITED. The booths look different on the surface but share pipes, wires, conveyor belts, and ducts below the floor.

Below ground is one enormous unstable machine room containing several model engines, GPU racks, node graphs, prompt hoppers, adapters, queue lights, a credit meter, a billing slot, policy gates, moderation scanners, region locks, version switches, and retry chutes. The machinery is complex but mechanically understandable, with labeled plates, warning stickers, receipt paper, cables, gears, server racks, and patched connectors.

At the far left, a neat source package enters the machine on a conveyor: folders, reference images, a schema card, notes, audio waveform, and a tag labeled JOB. Provider-specific adapters reshape the package along the route. One gate visibly rejects a card because the prompt is too long. One gate demands credits. One gate is marked REGION. One moderation scanner overreacts to an ordinary cardboard box.

At the far right, a small accepted-output tray contains one clean cinematic frame, one article diagram, one audio waveform, and one checked production card. Beneath it is an overflowing waste bin filled with failed generations, duplicate faces, broken motion frames, obsolete model cards, expired credits, and rejected prompts.

The scene should be funny, specific, crowded, and legible, with visible seams and physical machinery. Warm newsprint cream, carbon black, faded industrial blue, safety orange, receipt gray, a little warning red, and minor purple accents. No generic purple glow, no chrome humanoid robots, no influencer desk, no photorealistic AI woman, no corporate gradient, no real provider logos, no headline text.

## Composition

- Wide cinematic editorial illustration, target 21:9 or 16:9 master.
- Center the underground machine.
- Keep source package, central machine, and accepted-output tray inside a 4:3 safe crop.
- Keep all essential storytelling away from the outer 8 percent of the frame.
- Small labels may appear as environmental detail, but the image must work without reading them.
- Leave enough calm space near the upper center for the article layout to breathe, without adding title text.

## Desired finish

Hand-drawn ink, screen tone, paper texture, editorial collage, mechanical cutaway, satirical consumer-report energy. Detailed enough to reward inspection, clean enough to read at article width.

## Negative direction

Avoid:

- slick concept-art rendering;
- cinematic science-fiction control rooms;
- neon cyberpunk;
- brains made of circuits;
- robots typing at laptops;
- floating holograms;
- realistic brand logos;
- attractive generated characters as the focus;
- vaporwave or generic AI marketing style;
- illegible wall of tiny text.

## Deliverables

- wide master;
- article hero WebP and AVIF using current site conventions;
- 1200 x 630 social derivative;
- 4:3 mobile-safe crop only when the current image component cannot art-direct responsively;
- prompt, model, provider, generation date, edit history, dimensions, MIME, checksum, alt text, and credit in the asset manifest.
