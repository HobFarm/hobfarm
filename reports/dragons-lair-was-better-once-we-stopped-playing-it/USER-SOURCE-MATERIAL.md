# Dragon’s Lair article: user source material

The user supplied the article brief and the publication order. Personal recollections are treated as first-person memory, not generalized arcade history.

## Required personal facts preserved

- Dragon’s Lair was remembered as the expensive, impossible game and the only clearly remembered fifty-cent cabinet.
- The author’s peer group treated it as a waste of quarters.
- Preferred cabinets included Spy Hunter, Rampage, Gauntlet, Ghosts ’n Goblins, Altered Beast, and LED Storm.
- LED Storm became a repeated competition with a friend.
- Pac-Man and Donkey Kong were present but were not the author’s focus.
- Atari came home first and Nintendo later.
- Street Fighter II and Mortal Kombat remained important arcade landmarks in the early 1990s.
- Mortal Kombat was encountered in the arcade before the home ports.
- Wolfenstein 3D, Doom, and Doom modding made the computer more interesting than the arcade.
- Modern redemption arcades feel like slot-machine floors for children. The article keeps this as a personal impression and does not turn it into a legal or clinical claim.
- The Pinball Hall of Fame in Las Vegas remains a positive counterexample.
- The ending returns to a later conversation about Don Bluth and a full Dragon’s Lair playthrough watched on YouTube.
