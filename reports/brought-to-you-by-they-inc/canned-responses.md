## General

I wrote the long version here: https://hob.farm/articles/brought-to-you-by-they-inc/

The short version is that I want the source, scope, and causal chain before I accept a political payload.

## “This doesn’t cover everything.”

Correct. It is a thirty-year personal field report and a map of the recurring process, not a complete theory of media, politics, psychology, or propaganda. An omitted example does not excuse a faction, and an included example does not make unlike events equivalent.

## “So you think all news is fake?”

No. Evidence still matters, some reporting is much better than other reporting, and research shows that people can generally distinguish true news from false news. The article is about how selection, repetition, incentives, identity, and memory can bend that judgment between an event and the confident factoid carried into ordinary conversation.

## “You’re doing both-sides politics.”

I use equal inspection, not equal blame. Every faction has to name its sources and preserve scope, but the result does not have to come out even. Differences in truth, power, reach, and harm remain part of the record.

## “You only criticized my side.”

The examples follow the evidence and my own thirty-year vantage point. They are not a quota system. Show me the omitted example and its recoverable sources; if it reveals the same mechanism or corrects a factual error, it belongs in the continuing research. Its absence does not make the claims already documented disappear.

## “Which side are you on?”

I am not neutral between every claim. I am independent of your team.

## “You just don’t care.”

Sometimes I care about the underlying issue and still refuse the delivery. “I don’t care” means this subject does not automatically receive my limited attention because a feed, pundit, or coworker brought it to me.

## “Why call it bullshit before you know the truth?”

I am judging the package: missing source, missing scope, missing relevance, or a conclusion much broader than the evidence. Calling that package bullshit does not claim perfect knowledge of the whole event.

## “Who are ‘they’?”

That is the first question. If the source is recoverable, name it. If it is not, the anonymous authority is doing more work than the evidence.

## AI

AI has real labor and environmental costs, and it has real uses. The meaningful questions concern particular tasks, firms, facilities, cooling systems, grids, water sources, permits, and who receives the benefit. “AI destroys everything” and “AI fixes everything” are both membership products.

## Elon Musk

Using software or owning a car does not enroll me in its chief executive’s politics. I can use a tool, criticize the company, and refuse the rival group’s complete story about what my use means.

## Ukraine flag

A profile flag can express sincere solidarity. It can also function as an identity signal in a political cluster. The article says both and explicitly rejects treating one symbol as a perfect party classifier.

## Epstein records

The crimes and institutional failures were real. A name’s appearance in a contact book, email, photograph, allegation, flight record, or investigative file does not establish the same thing in every case. The document, provenance, context, corroboration, and conduct matter.

## 2020 and January 6

The article does not call the events equivalent. It compares how teams select words such as protest, riot, uprising, and insurrection to preserve an approved meaning.

## Lettuce

The July 2026 Cyclospora outbreak was real and serious. The official investigation concerned recalled Taylor Farms de Mexico iceberg lettuce, Taco Bell exposure in nine states, and food-service distribution in 27 states. I was not eating at Taco Bell, and Nevada appeared on neither official state list. The warning I received had lost the supplier, product, recall, dates, geography, and unresolved laboratory evidence while preserving a conclusion about the president. That transformation is the whole article.
