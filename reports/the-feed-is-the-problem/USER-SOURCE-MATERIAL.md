# The Feed Is the Problem: user source material

Recorded August 14 and revised August 17, 2026. These points came from the author's task packets and remain first-person experience or interpretation, not findings supplied by outside research.

- The author built subject folders in Thunderbird to find material without relying on social-media discovery.
- Phys.org delivered the doomscrolling-at-work story through that reader.
- The article is a direct follow-up to **Hit the Source Directly**.
- The author's working model is that a platform can record distress-driven attention as successful engagement, then return that behavior as a distribution and production signal.
- The author initially wondered why therapy occupied more public attention. The final argument moves upstream and treats ranked feeds as one system that can contribute to, amplify, or prolong distress. It does not use treatment or medication statistics.
- RSS is presented as source control, not as proof that a source is trustworthy.
- A ranked public feed is not equivalent to a mutual relationship, including a mutual relationship maintained online.
- The author almost never scrolls ranked social feeds, blocks platform advertising, and uses YouTube or social platforms to retrieve a specific video, account, announcement, product, or source.
- Material found through RSS often reaches the author before later newsletters and social promotion turn it into a hook.
- HobFarm publishes on its own site and uses social distribution when useful rather than treating a platform monetization quota as the client.
- PetaPixel delivered the Meta trial story through RSS while the article was already built and scheduled.
