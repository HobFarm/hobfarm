## Evidence classes

The finished article distinguishes five kinds of material:

- Verified facts come from dated government, utility, court, institutional, or peer-reviewed records.
- Field observations come from HobFarm's 2010 photographs and first-person visit. They do not establish water chemistry, disease, or exact elevation at the moment of capture.
- Screening calculations come from the committed model and its declared assumptions.
- Engineering inferences identify plausible configurations or consequences that still need design work.
- Proposals describe the desired regional system, target-study band, pilot sequence, and governance conditions. They are not claims about an approved project.

## Model boundaries

The committed model is deterministic. It converts the Bahrain capacity and actual-production references in code, interpolates the 1995 area-capacity table, and calculates every number shown in the article graphics. Its base case uses a -240 ft NGVD 1929 study elevation, 35.5 ppt lake target, 45 percent steady-state recovery, 90 percent plant availability, 95 percent finished-water conveyance efficiency, and 5.6 ft of annual net evaporation.

The 90 ppt transition feed and 25 percent transition recovery are declared planning sensitivities, not same-day field measurements or vendor designs. The 20 percent drainage-return response is also a sensitivity. The model does not resolve seasonal operations, membrane selection, intake ecology, exact current chemistry, parcel impacts, conveyance alignment, treatment residual chemistry, power contracts, marketable mineral yields, cost, or treaty terms.

The -230 ft checkpoint produces 1,943,096 AFY of treated inlet in the final interpolation, close to the task's 1,942,745 AFY reference. The small difference comes from using the exact survey table and the model's full-precision conversion instead of the report's rounded 1.306 MAFY evaporation estimate.

## Remaining feasibility receipts

The article is ready as a sourced proposal, but a real project would still need current water assays; seasonal gauge and evaporation series; surveyed shoreline and parcel GIS; intake and pipeline studies in Mexico; tribal, land, and habitat consultation; treatment and membrane trials on each feed; salt and contaminant residual plans; power, transmission, and heat audits; customer and Colorado River accounting agreements; capital and operating costs; mineral purity and offtake evidence; and a specific binational agreement.

The museum provenance and creator-unverified label should travel with any future reuse of the historical scans. They must not be recaptioned as author-owned photographs or stripped of their archive context.
