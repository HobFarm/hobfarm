# User Source Material

## Current article idea

This article follows a scene in *Cheech & Chong's Nice Dreams* in which Paul Reubens's character Howie talks about the future of rock and roll.

The useful line is:

> Bruce Springsteen's fucking the whole thing up.

Howie's answer is:

> New Wave!

Tommy Chong barely seems to care about or follow the music argument. The article should notice that mismatch, then ask why Howie would call New Wave the future of rock and roll in 1981.

The article should not spend a long time explaining the scene's cocaine context. Chong is referring back to the cocaine they used under the table when he asks whether Howie has something for sale. That is not the article's main joke.

## User's organizing idea

Brian Eno is the main connective tissue in the selected mesh:

- Roxy Music;
- John Cage and experimental-composition influence;
- David Bowie;
- Robert Fripp;
- Talking Heads and David Byrne;
- Devo.

Connected personnel and production paths include:

- Fripp;
- Adrian Belew;

Giorgio Moroder is another major part of the story:

- electronic pulse and synthesizer-driven pop;
- Donna Summer;
- Japan;
- Blondie;
- Bowie;
- film scores;
- *The NeverEnding Story*;
- Daft Punk's "Giorgio by Moroder."

The article should expand beyond one narrow New Wave sound. Other branches were appearing at the same time:

- goth;
- post-punk;
- synth-pop;
- New Romantic;
- industrial;
- art-pop;
- related art-house scenes.

Japan are a useful before-and-after example because their early records do not sound like the band they became. "Life in Tokyo" and the later albums make the shift visible.

The transatlantic exchange matters. Many key artists were British or European, but American nodes include:

- Talking Heads in New York;
- Devo in Akron;
- Oingo Boingo in Los Angeles;
- Paul Reubens and the Groundlings;
- American funk, R&B, soul, television, film, and performance culture.

David Byrne is Scottish-born and American-raised. Use source-accurate wording.

## Paul Reubens branch

Following Paul Reubens helps explain why Howie's speech may have reflected a real cultural position:

- Reubens and the Groundlings appear in the Cheech & Chong orbit;
- *Cheech & Chong's Next Movie* includes Pee-wee;
- Phil Hartman is part of the Groundlings and Pee-wee network;
- Reubens later approaches Mark Mothersbaugh for *Pee-wee's Big Adventure*;
- Danny Elfman scores the film;
- Mark Mothersbaugh scores *Pee-wee's Playhouse*;
- Devo and Oingo Boingo become direct screen-music connections.

Do not claim private intent without evidence. The point is that the line fits the world Reubens occupied.
