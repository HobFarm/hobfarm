# Visual Plan: New Wave Mesh

## Visual principle

The old infographic tried to make one image hold the entire thesis, chronology, genre map, visual language, and legacy. The article should do that work in sequence.

Build several focused graphics. Each one has one job.

Use period atmosphere sparingly. The visual language can borrow from 1979 to 1983 record sleeves, television graphics, restaurant menus, patch bays, studio diagrams, graph paper, Letraset, photocopy texture, and early video typography. Do not default to a neon skyline, grid floor, chrome type, or generic synthwave wallpaper.

## Required graphic 4: XTC to Gabriel

**Asset ID:** `04-xtc-gabriel-drum-transfer`

**Purpose:** Explain one concrete technical transfer.

**Flow:**

```text
XTC
Drums and Wires, 1979
Steve Lillywhite, producer
Hugh Padgham, engineer
        |
        v
Peter Gabriel 3, 1980
Phil Collins on "Intruder"
room microphones + gate
        |
        v
the drum sound becomes composition
        |
        v
a production language spreads through the 1980s
        |
        v
Primus covers both branches
"Making Plans For Nigel" + "Intruder"
```

**Mechanism panel:**

1. capture room ambience;
2. compress or emphasize the room signal;
3. gate cuts the tail abruptly;
4. the drum becomes huge and unnaturally controlled.

Do not claim the diagram represents every studio's exact signal chain.

**Caption idea:** The people who made *Drums and Wires* carried a drum experiment into Peter Gabriel's third album. "Intruder" turned the treatment into the structure of the song.

**Alt text:** A flow diagram connects XTC's *Drums and Wires* through producer Steve Lillywhite and engineer Hugh Padgham to Peter Gabriel's "Intruder," then shows room microphones and a noise gate creating a large drum sound with an abruptly cut tail.

## Required graphic 6: The future arrives in pieces

**Asset ID:** `06-new-wave-timeline`

**Purpose:** Keep chronology visible as the network expands.

**Suggested milestones:**

- 1972 or 1973: optional Springsteen and Cheech & Chong bill, only if verified;
- 1973: Fripp and Eno collaboration;
- 1977: Bowie/Eno period and Donna Summer's "I Feel Love";
- 1978: Devo with Eno, Talking Heads with Eno;
- 1979: XTC *Drums and Wires*, Japan "Life in Tokyo" and *Quiet Life*;
- 1980: *Peter Gabriel 3*, *Remain in Light*, *Cheech & Chong's Next Movie*;
- 1981: *Nice Dreams*, *Discipline*, *Tin Drum*, MTV launch, *My Life in the Bush of Ghosts*;
- 1982: *Beat*, Peter Gabriel's fourth album, "Cat People";
- 1984: *Three of a Perfect Pair*, *The NeverEnding Story*;
- 1985: *Pee-wee's Big Adventure*;
- 1986: *Pee-wee's Playhouse*;
- 1992: Primus *Miscellaneous Debris*;
- 1998: Primus *Rhinoplasty*;
- 2013: Daft Punk, "Giorgio by Moroder."

Use exact dates only after verification.

**Caption idea:** Howie's 1981 speech lands near the center of the transformation, after the experiments have begun and just before their methods become ordinary mass culture.

**Alt text:** A timeline from the early 1970s to 2013 places the *Nice Dreams* New Wave scene among Eno, Moroder, XTC, Peter Gabriel, King Crimson, Japan, MTV, Pee-wee, Primus, and Daft Punk milestones.

## Asset production requirements

For every final graphic:

- create a master source;
- create web derivatives;
- record dimensions and checksums;
- use real typography;
- do not ask an image model to render labels;
- preserve high contrast;
- test at 390 pixels;
- include caption and alt text;
- create a text equivalent;
- verify every date and relationship;
- record local path, R2 key, public URL, MIME, byte size, and cache response.

## Visual stopping rule

The article has enough graphics when the reader can understand:

- why Reubens belongs in the story;
- how the network differs from a genre list;
- how ideas moved geographically;
- how one technical process traveled;
- when the major events happened;
- how the article returns to Springsteen.

Do not add another graphic only because another band exists.
