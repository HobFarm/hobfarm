# Article Packet: Bruce Springsteen Is Fucking the Whole Thing Up

## Source idea

In *Cheech & Chong's Nice Dreams*, Paul Reubens's Howie interrupts a restaurant table to ask about the future of rock and roll. He says Bruce Springsteen is "fucking the whole thing up," then announces the answer: "New Wave!"

Tommy Chong is barely following the musical argument. The movie leaves the subject there.

The article follows it.

## Working thesis

Howie's speech lands in 1981, when "New Wave" still named an unsettled field of art-rock, punk, post-punk, electronic, dance, theatrical, and visual mutations. The future did not come from one band or one country. It moved through a dense transatlantic mesh of musicians, producers, engineers, studios, techniques, visual cultures, comedy scenes, and later descendants.

Brian Eno is the selected mesh's strongest connective tissue. Giorgio Moroder is another major router. David Bowie, Robert Fripp, Kraftwerk, Talking Heads, Devo, XTC, Peter Gabriel, Japan, King Crimson, Oingo Boingo, Mark Mothersbaugh, Danny Elfman, Paul Reubens, Primus, and Daft Punk make the network audible.

Howie was right that the methods would become the future. He was wrong that Springsteen had to leave.

## Scope

This is not:

- a definitive New Wave history;
- a ranked list of bands;
- a complete genre taxonomy;
- a claim that Eno invented everything;
- a claim that every connected artist sounded alike;
- a claim that Reubens privately believed every word Howie said;
- an anti-Springsteen article;
- a 1980s nostalgia roundup.

It is one story the evidence lets us tell.

### 11. Bruce Springsteen remains a problem

Return to Howie.

The final understanding:

- New Wave did become a major part of rock and pop's future.
- Its methods spread farther than the label.
- Springsteen did not stop it.
- Springsteen also adapted to the visual and production culture of the decade.
- Several futures coexisted.

Possible final landing:

> The future arrived. Bruce Springsteen was still in it.
