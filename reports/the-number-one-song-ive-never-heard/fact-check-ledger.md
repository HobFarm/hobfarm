- Official Charts records “Rein Me In” at 19 nonconsecutive weeks at number one, passing a singles-chart longevity record held since 1953.
- The Guardian documented that many readers said they had never heard the record. The article does not claim that nobody knew it.
- Official Charts says the current chart combines physical sales, downloads, audio streams, and video streams. Exact conversion rules remain a publication-day recheck.
- Spotify Radio starts from an artist, album, or song. Spotify’s separate recommendation explanation documents taste-profile and behavioral inputs.
- YouTube says the current video is the main signal for the next-video recommendation. It says watch history is the main homepage signal.
- BBC history records the first *Top of the Pops* broadcast on January 1, 1964. Official Charts records the end of the regular weekly run in 2006.
- Nirvana’s performance was recorded November 27 and broadcast November 28, 1991. Nirvana’s official archive confirms the performance in *Live! Tonight! Sold Out!!*.
- The Nirvana still is an author-supplied screenshot from the fan archive upload at `6s4KXiXVFAI`. It is used only to illustrate the discussed performance. The factual receipt remains Nirvana's official *Live! Tonight! Sold Out!!* archive.
- Official Charts dates the Breeders’ “Cannonball” to August 1993. The article corrects the author’s tentative 1994 recollection.
- The official video and production records support the credited directors for “Life Signs,” “oh baby,” “Gosh,” “Cirrus,” and “Horse.”
