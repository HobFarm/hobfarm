## Editorial interpretation

The article's central comparison is an inference built from the verified cases: arid places preserve evidence of water across several clocks. Lake Mead is a managed reservoir, Tule Springs is a fossil wetland record, Lake Cahuilla is a recurring basin lake, the Salton Sea is a historic refilling triggered by canal failure and flood, and Lake Chad is a shallow lake-wetland system. Their mechanisms are not interchangeable.

### New Liverpool Salt Company photograph record

The supplied provenance sheet identifies the dry saltworks photograph as the New Liverpool Salt Company at Salton, California, and dates it to before 1905. It asks that the Coachella Valley Water District receive credit and identifies the Ruth White Peters Collection at the Indio Library. The photographer field is blank. The sheet bears a February 3, 1998 record date and several archival-number markings, including a handwritten `#1223`; that date belongs to the record, not the photograph. The accompanying historical caption describes the plant's later submergence, the Liverpool salt trademark, and George W. Dubrow's operation of the works. Those additional ownership details are preserved here but are not needed for the article's argument.

The sheet documents the dry view only. It does not identify the flooded photograph, so that scan's item number, exposure date, and photographer remain unassigned.
