# Hit the Source Directly: user source material

Recorded August 12, 2026, before drafting. These points came directly from the author and should remain identified as first-person memory or current practice. They are not claims established by outside reporting.

- The author first learned about RSS while publishing videos around 2006.
- The author remembers adding RSS feeds to Thunderbird or another email-style application.
- This period is covered in the HobFarm article **The Future Was Already There**.
- The author is now publishing HobFarm articles on a near-daily cadence.
- The articles cover many subjects rather than one narrow topic.
- Some articles produce supporting images and video when the material and production energy support them.
- The research, article construction, arguments, and conceptual connections consume most of the author's production time.
- RSS should become HobFarm's recommended direct subscription path for readers who want every article.
- The author's working mechanism is: **RSS bypasses the engagement factory.**
- The author's useful phrasing is: **hit the source directly.**
- The author sees algorithmic slop as an incentive problem. Engagement systems promote rage-bait, repetition, synthetic filler, and other material optimized to provoke or retain attention.
- RSS does not need to decide whether a new HobFarm article is competitive with whatever is trending that day.
- A reader subscribes to the publication, and the reader receives what the publication releases.
- A personal AI system can sit downstream from RSS and help filter or summarize material selected by the reader. The AI does not need to inherit a social platform's source-selection logic.

## Evidence boundary

The article may connect these memories to documented history, software behavior, and current publishing systems, but it should not add exact screens, subscription lists, feed URLs, or workflow steps that the author did not supply.
